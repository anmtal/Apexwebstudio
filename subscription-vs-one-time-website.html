Website Subscription vs One-Time Build

Two ways to pay for a custom website: a low monthly subscription with no money down, or a traditional one-time build fee. Here is a clear, side-by-side comparison to help you choose — and where each one makes sense.

When the subscription wins

If you want to launch without a big cheque, keep cash in the business, and never think about hosting, security, or updates again, the subscription is the simplest path. It bundles everything into one predictable monthly fee, and the price drops after the first year. This is the right choice for most new and small businesses.

When the one-time build wins

If you have budget set aside and want the lowest total cost over several years, the one-time build makes sense. You pay for the site upfront and keep a lighter monthly maintenance plan. Established businesses replacing an older site often prefer this route.

QuestionsSubscription vs One-Time — FAQ

Is a website subscription cheaper than paying upfront?

It depends on your timeline. A subscription has no large upfront cost — you start from $149/month with nothing down — so it is cheaper to launch. A one-time build costs more upfront (from $499) but less over several years. Most small businesses that want to preserve cash flow choose the subscription.

Do I own my website on the subscription plan?

On the subscription plan, your site, hosting, and maintenance are bundled into the monthly fee for the 12-month term. With the one-time build, you pay for the site upfront and keep a lighter monthly maintenance plan. Either way you own your domain and content; we can walk you through the specifics for your situation.

What happens after the 12-month subscription term?

After the first year the monthly price drops (for example, the Landing plan falls from $149 to $99/month) and continues to include hosting, security, backups, and updates. There is no forced re-build; the site keeps running and being maintained.

Which option is better for a brand-new business?

For a brand-new business, the subscription is usually the better fit — it removes the upfront cost barrier, includes everything you need to launch, and lets you scale the site as you grow. Established businesses with budget on hand sometimes prefer the one-time build to lower long-term cost.
